and university-adjacent wellness settings. The current study build combines
location-aware discovery of nearby health resources, curated health education,
a personal-health workspace surface, and persistent access to urgent support
while minimizing accounts, analytics, and location tracking.

The main contributions of this work are intended to include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A mobile health navigation architecture for context-aware nearby
    resource discovery that avoids device GPS tracking in the current study
    build, using town-level geocoding and OpenStreetMap queries instead.
    \item A privacy-oriented product posture without user accounts or
    third-party analytics, with a personal-health workspace designed for
    local-first storage as implementation matures beyond demo data.
    \item A co-design-oriented interface integrating nearby care discovery,
    personal health organization, health education, and urgent-support access.

\subsection{Online Health Information Seeking and Fragmented Access}
A large literature documents that people routinely seek health information online,
yet information is scattered across websites, portals, and social platforms and is
often difficult to evaluate~\cite{Jia2021OHIS,DiazHernandez2024HealthInfo,Zhao2022OlderOHIS}.
Recent national survey evidence further shows that social media is a common venue
for health-related engagement among {US} adults, even as many users distrust the
accuracy of what they encounter~\cite{Pedroso2026SocialMedia}. Parallel policy
commentary argues that digital access programs are tightly coupled to digital
health equity for low-income populations facing structural barriers to
care~\cite{Fitzpatrick2025DigitalEquity}. Community-oriented ``social
prescribing'' approaches likewise aim to connect people to non-medical supports,
but depend on reliable local resource networks~\cite{Lancet2025SocialPrescribing}.
Taken together, these works motivate mobile \emph{navigation} interfaces that
centralize discovery of nearby resources and trusted education without assuming
that users can assemble care pathways unaided.

\subsection{Barriers to Mobile and Digital Health Engagement}
Qualitative and review evidence identifies recurring barriers to digital health
use among underserved and culturally diverse populations, including access,
literacy, language, trust, and usability~\cite{Whitehead2023Barriers}.
Within integrated youth services, interest-holders report that mHealth can reduce
barriers to in-person care while introducing privacy, design, and sustainability
challenges that call for co-designed solutions~\cite{Ding2025mHealth}.
Digital literacy is increasingly treated as a social determinant of health,
especially for people with serious mental illness who may otherwise be excluded
from digital tools~\cite{Dwyer2025TechUse}. These findings inform CWC~Health's
emphasis on plain information architecture, source-labeled Learn content, and
minimal account/analytics surface area in the study build.

\subsection{Co-Design and Participatory Digital Health}
Community-based participatory research and related co-production traditions argue
that equitable decision-making with community partners improves relevance and
trust~\cite{Opara2025CBPR,PapastavrouBrooks2023Principles}.
Lived-experience co-leadership is likewise framed as essential for redesigning
mental and substance-use care systems that remain fragmented and
stigma-laden~\cite{Epp2025CoLeadership}. At the same time, critics caution that
``participatory'' labels can be assimilated into institutional innovation without
genuine power sharing~\cite{Veldmeijer2025Participatory}.
Empirical co-design of digital tools for substance-use contexts---including
adaptation of App4Independence for opioid use disorder and formative work on a
wellness-enhanced contingency-management smartphone
concept---illustrates how lived experts and clinicians jointly shape feasibility,
safety, and feature priorities~\cite{Darcey2024A4iO,Deshais2026WECM}.
CWC~Health builds on this lineage as a co-designed health-\emph{navigation}
prototype: it targets discoverability of nearby care, personal-health
organization surfaces, education, and urgent support under a data-minimization
posture, rather than clinical decision support or generative AI advice.

\subsection{Design Goals}
CWC Health is designed as a \emph{health-navigation} application rather than a
clinical decision-support system: it helps users find care resources, organize
locally held health information, read source-labeled education content, and
reach urgent support quickly, without diagnosing conditions or recommending
treatments. Design goals emphasize (i)~data minimization (no accounts, no
third-party analytics), (ii)~clear information architecture discoverable in
co-design sessions, and (iii)~optional live resource discovery that does not
depend on continuous location tracking. The Flutter study prototype implements
this shell and a live Nearby path; several personal-health and offline-directory
behaviors remain design targets documented in the project specification and are
called out explicitly below.

\subsection{Application Structure}
The study build is a cross-platform Flutter application with four primary bottom
tabs---Nearby, My Health, Learn, and More---plus a persistent Help~Now control
in the application header so urgent actions remain one tap away from any tab.
Nearby is the default landing surface. Compile-time flags gate network-facing
behavior: \texttt{LIVE\_NEARBY} (default off) replaces the static Nearby demo
list with live OpenStreetMap listings, and \texttt{HELP\_NOW\_LIVE} (default
off) enables device dialer/SMS launches for selected national support numbers.
In the current study build, My~Health and Learn still present curated sample
content for co-design feedback; More routes to placeholder settings and support
pages, including an erase affordance that currently explains that personal
health records are not yet stored on the device.

\subsection{Privacy and Data Minimization}
As implemented, the prototype does not create user accounts, store login
credentials, or include third-party analytics software development kits. Live
Nearby retains only a last-success listing cache and a town geocode point on
device---no identity payload and no GPS trail. Help~Now national dialers remain
off unless an explicit study build enables them, reducing accidental live crisis
calls during demos. Intended MVP privacy controls from the feature specification
(optional PIN for My~Health, encrypted on-device personal records, and a working
one-tap erase of saved personal data) are \emph{not} claimed as shipped in the
present study build; the paper will update these statements when the application
repository implements them.

\subsection{Location-Aware Nearby Services}
When \texttt{LIVE\_NEARBY} is enabled, Nearby geocodes a fixed town name (New
Brunswick, NJ, in the current v1 engineering demo) through Nominatim and queries
OpenStreetMap Overpass mirrors for pharmacies, clinics, and urgent care within a
local radius. An optional Google Places attempt may soft-fail to OpenStreetMap
when no billed key is provided. The live path does not request device location
permissions and does not use background location APIs. Results are deduplicated,
time-stamped, and cached locally; if all network sources fail, the interface
shows an error with retry and deliberately avoids falling back to invented demo
listings so users cannot confuse synthetic rows with real places. Directions
open OpenStreetMap or native geo links rather than embedding a third-party map
SDK. Flag-off builds keep a static New~Brunswick demo list for meetings and
automated tests.

\subsection{On-Device Personal Health Information}
My~Health exposes appointments, medications, providers, and a wallet-card view
as co-design surfaces. In the current study build these entries are sample
constants rather than a persisted personal store; the interface states that
My~Health is not PIN-locked yet. Learn presents a small set of offline article
topics with explicit source labels (for example, public-health agencies) and
does not generate AI answers. The longer-term design target is a local-first
personal-health workspace with optional protection and erasure---again, to be
described as implemented only after the application code supports it.
